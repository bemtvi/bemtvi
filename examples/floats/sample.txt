Floating-windows playground — open floats and watch them paint on top.

A float is a window positioned by absolute coordinates ON TOP of the tiled
layout, rather than tiled into it. It steals no space from its neighbours: it
paints over them. This is how plugins draw hover, completion docs, keymap-hint
popups, fuzzy-finders, notifications, and bordered vim.ui prompts.

A hint float opened on startup (top of the screen, single border, titled
"nxvim floats"). It did not steal focus — your cursor is still down here.

Try, in order:
  1. :FloatHello     a centered, rounded, titled float ("hello from a float").
                     It takes focus — the cursor is now inside the float.
  2. :FloatClose     (or \x) close it again; the buffer beneath is intact.
  3. :FloatStack     two overlapping floats. The higher zindex sits on top,
                     even though it was opened first — stacking is by zindex.
  4. 10G then :FloatCursor   jump down, then open a float at the cursor cell.

Each float binds THIS buffer, so it shows the same text — the border, title,
and position are what mark it as a float. A float steals no space: the lines
here never reflow when one opens.
