This is the MAIN editor area — and it has its OWN tab pages.

Around it are two permanent docks: a left EXPLORER side bar and a bottom
TERMINAL tray. Every region — main and each dock — carries its own independent
set of vim tab pages, each drawn as its own tabline.

Each region's tabline:

    * main's tabline    the top bar of the editor (this file's tabs live here)
    * the left dock     the first row of the left band ("EXPLORER" + its tabs)
    * the bottom dock   the first row of the bottom band ("TERMINAL" + its tabs)

Tab operations act on the FOCUSED region. Try it:

    <C-w><C-w>h     focus the LEFT dock      (j/k/l → bottom / top / right)
    :tabnew         add a tab — to the focused region ONLY
    :T 3            shortcut: three :tabnew in the focused region
    gt   /   gT     cycle the focused region's tabs (next / previous)
    <C-w><C-w>l     cross back to main; now gt cycles MAIN's tabs

Switch a region's tab with the MOUSE, too:

    left-click a tabline cell   switch THAT region to the clicked tab and move
                                focus into it — main's bar or any dock's strip

Closing tabs:

    :tabclose       close the focused tab
                    (closing a dock's LAST tab folds the dock away;
                     the main area always keeps at least one tab)

The regions never interfere: a stack of tabs in the bottom tray leaves main's
tabline — and the left dock's — exactly as they were.
